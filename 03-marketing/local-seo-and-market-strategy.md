# Local SEO and Market Strategy: Decision Memo

**Status:** draft for Brad and Sarah to decide
**Owner:** Brad
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-28

This memo exists to make three decisions before any money or build time is spent: how to think about geography, how wide to target, and which vertical to lead with. It is written to be argued with. Every market claim is either cited or flagged as an estimate. Exact search-volume numbers are not guessed here; where they are needed, the memo says so and points to how to get them.

## The setup, in one paragraph

The pin is a home address in Frisco, ZIP 75033, which is the Denton County side of Frisco (Frisco spans both Collin and Denton counties). This is affluent, high-density small-business territory in what local realtors call the Platinum Corridor. It is also next door to Celina, Prosper, and Aubrey, which are among the fastest-growing cities in the United States and are in the middle of a housing and construction boom. Sarah and Brad have no existing warm network in any single industry. The firm can deliver bookkeeping to anyone, anywhere, over Zoom and a portal.

## Decision 0: the question underneath the question (serve anywhere vs. get found)

Brad's instinct: if we can serve anyone digitally, why anchor to a town at all? This is the most important thing to settle, so here it is straight.

**"Who you can serve" and "who can find you" are two different problems.**

- **Serving** is unlimited. Remote bookkeeping works for a client in Frisco, Houston, or Ohio. Take any client who comes, from anywhere. Nothing in this memo limits that.
- **Getting found** is not unlimited, and you do not get to choose to be found everywhere. Google's local results (the map pack) anchor to your verified pin. Independent testing is clear: the service areas you type into a Google Business Profile are visual only and do not extend where you rank. A Frisco pin cannot rank for "bookkeeper Houston." (Sterling Sky research, cited below.)

So the real fork is not "local clients vs. anywhere clients." It is **how you get discovered at all**:

- **Local discovery** (map pack + local website pages) is a small, winnable pond. Competition is limited by geography, and "bookkeeper near me" carries real buyer intent. This is the one channel where a two-person firm holds a structural edge, which is exactly why the business plan ranks it first.
- **Geography-agnostic discovery** (rank nationally for "bookkeeping services") throws that edge away. You would compete against the entire internet: national ad-buyers like Collective, content farms, and every remote bookkeeper in the country. That is the pond where Bench raised over $100 million and still went bankrupt, and where Botkeeper and QuickBooks Live died. It is the most expensive, most crowded way in, and it is precisely what the plan says not to do.

**Conclusion for Decision 0:** anchor discovery locally because that is where you can actually win, serve anyone who comes regardless of location, and treat broader or out-of-area clients as upside the website earns over time, never as the foundation. Going national-first is the one clearly wrong answer.

## Your pin, graded

**The good:** Frisco 75033 is money. High household income, dense small-business base, and a business-formation boom next door. There is demand here.

**The hard:** the head term "bookkeeper Frisco" (and "Plano," "McKinney") is contested by established tax-and-bookkeeping firms with hundreds of reviews (for example Precision Tax and Bookkeeping at 200-plus) and a national franchise (Paramount). Beating a 200-review incumbent for the generic term is slow, review-gated, and low-confidence. The plan's own estimate for that channel is 3 to 9 months at best.

**The opening (this is the part that changes the plan):** your Denton County position puts you on the doorstep of Celina, Prosper, and Aubrey, three of the fastest-growing cities in the country, booming with new housing and therefore crawling with construction and home-services trades. Bookkeeping competition in those specific suburbs is far thinner than in core Frisco or Plano. No local firm with hundreds of reviews owns "bookkeeper Prosper" or "bookkeeper Celina" the way Precision owns Frisco. That is a smaller, newer, less-defended pond, full of exactly the client type the plan wants, sitting inside your ranking radius.

## Decision 1: geographic targeting (pick one)

### Option A: Own your immediate suburb (Frisco core)

Target "bookkeeper Frisco" and the Frisco/Plano core.

- **Pros:** highest raw search volume; it is literally where you live; most affluent clients.
- **Cons:** the most defended terms in the metro; you are the newcomer with zero reviews against 200-review incumbents and a franchise; slowest path; you are fighting on the incumbents' turf. This is the hard mode.

### Option B: The north growth corridor (Celina, Prosper, Aubrey, Little Elm, plus Frisco) — recommended

Anchor the Frisco pin, but aim your website and profile at the under-defended booming suburbs to your north and west.

- **Pros:** far thinner competition; explosive new-business and construction growth (your primary vertical is physically concentrated here); "bookkeeping for contractors in Prosper/Celina" is a term you can realistically rank for and own; the growth means the client base is expanding under you, not fixed. Best odds-adjusted return.
- **Cons:** individually smaller suburbs mean lower per-term volume than the Frisco head term (you win by covering several); some of these clients are newer businesses, which the plan flags as lower-quality until they mature (manage with the screening scorecard, SOP-010).

### Option C: Geography-agnostic / national digital

Skip local, compete online for generic bookkeeping terms nationally.

- **Pros:** unlimited theoretical market; not capped by the pin.
- **Cons:** you surrender the only structural edge you have; you compete with national ad budgets and content farms; it is the graveyard channel (Bench, Botkeeper, QuickBooks Live); slow, expensive, low trust, no reviews to start. This is the trap the instinct leads to. Not recommended as the primary play.

**Recommendation: Option B.** Anchor Frisco, target the north corridor's under-served, fast-growing suburbs, and let broader/remote clients arrive as a bonus through website content and referrals. It is the only option that puts a winnable pond and your best vertical in the same place.

## Decision 2: the vertical to lead with (pick one to lead)

Leading a vertical means your homepage, your niche pages, and your one free tool all speak to that industry. You still accept good clients outside it; you just point the marketing at one.

### Home-services contractors (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, remodel, landscaping) — recommended

- **Pros:** highest pain and highest willingness to outsource bookkeeping; low specialist competition; and, decisively for you, the north-corridor construction boom means these businesses are being created around you right now. Geography and vertical line up perfectly. It is also a niche you can own in copy that the generalist tax firms ignore.
- **Cons:** their books are messier and more variable (more hours per client early); some are price-sensitive; job-costing adds complexity on larger ones. Manage with screening (prefer 2-plus-year-old businesses) and tier pricing.

### Professional services (law offices, consultants, agencies, real-estate brokers, medical/dental practices)

- **Pros:** clean books, stable, high retention, low hours per client, the capacity-friendly "ballast." Easiest to actually deliver.
- **Cons:** less acute pain, so a harder sell (many already have someone); more competition for their attention; lower urgency means longer sales cycles.

### Restaurants

- **Pros:** high need, high complexity they cannot handle themselves.
- **Cons:** roughly half close within five years, so built-in churn no service quality prevents; POS complexity; the plan caps them at 25 percent of the roster. Never a lead vertical.

**Recommendation: lead with home-services contractors,** because with no warm network to leverage, geography is your tiebreaker, and your geography is a construction boom. Accept clean professional-services clients opportunistically as the low-hour ballast. This is not either/or.

## The roster mix (so "lead with one" does not get misread)

Leading with contractors does not mean an all-contractor book. The healthy target mix from the plan:

- Home-services contractors: 40 to 50 percent (the lead)
- Professional services: 25 to 35 percent (the stable ballast)
- Restaurants: opportunistic only, capped at 25 percent

Lead generation points at contractors; the book fills out with whoever fits the screening scorecard.

## Honest data caveat (do not skip this)

This memo grades competition from live search results, which is real. It does **not** contain exact monthly search-volume numbers, because those cannot be pulled reliably from general search and the plan's own failure log warns against quoting plausible-but-unsourced numbers. Before final keyword targeting:

- Get real volumes from **Google Keyword Planner** (free with a Google Ads account, no ad spend required). Pull volumes for "bookkeeper [suburb]," "bookkeeping services [suburb]," and "bookkeeping for [contractor type] [suburb]" across Frisco, Prosper, Celina, Aubrey, Little Elm.
- That data confirms which suburbs and which contractor sub-niches are worth a page. Build pages against measured demand, not this memo's directional read.

If it would help, the next step can be a keyword-and-competition worksheet built from Keyword Planner once the Google Ads account exists.

## The honest risk (not softened)

Local SEO is slow and low-confidence at the start, 3 to 9 months to the first inbound client even done well. With no warm network, the first one to three clients most likely come from a reduced-fee warm start, an Upwork listing used to manufacture a first review, or a CPA referral, while the SEO asset matures underneath. Acquisition, not delivery, remains the real risk of this whole venture. This geography-plus-vertical choice gives you the best-odds version of the SEO bet; it does not make it fast.

## Recommended build sequence (once you decide)

1. Lock the Texas naming and keyword architecture and send the confirmatory inquiry to TSBPA (gate before any copy).
2. Set up the Google Business Profile as a service-area business, Frisco pin hidden, category "Bookkeeping service," service areas set to the corridor suburbs.
3. Build the website: one homepage plus a niche page per suburb, "bookkeeping for contractors in [Prosper / Celina / Aubrey / Little Elm / Frisco]."
4. Build one genuinely useful free tool or diagnostic aimed at contractors.
5. Stand up the review-request engine that fires after each clean close.
6. Open CPA referral outreach in the May to June window.

## Bottom line

Anchor local because it is the only pond you can win, but point that anchor at the under-defended, booming north corridor rather than the defended Frisco head term. Lead with home-services contractors because your geography is a construction boom and you have no warm network to override it. Serve anyone who comes, from anywhere, and let the website earn the broader clients over time. Do not go national-first; that is the one clearly losing move.
