# SOP-016: Offboarding

**Status:** active
**Owner:** Sarah
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Purpose

Ending an engagement cleanly, whoever ended it and however it ended.

This is the highest-risk moment in the whole relationship and it is the one most
firms have no checklist for. At offboarding the firm still holds access to a
client's financial system, still has a payment authorization armed against their
bank account, still holds their documents, and is about to stop paying attention.
Every one of those is a live exposure until somebody closes it deliberately.

It is also the moment a client decides what to say about you for the next several
years. A departing client who was handled well refers people. A departing client
who had to chase you for their files tells a different story, and the audience is
the same small local market either way.

## Trigger

Any of these:

- The client gives notice (30 days, per the engagement letter).
- The firm gives notice.
- Renewal screening (SOP-010) returns a decision not to continue.
- The engagement is terminated for cause: non-payment past the SOP-005 path,
  or a client asking for something to be recorded in a way we will not record it.

## The rule that governs everything below

**The client's books belong to the client.** Not to us, not contingent on
anything. We do not hold files, exports, or access hostage over an unpaid
balance, a disagreement, or a bad parting. Doing so is unprofessional, it is a
reputational disaster in a referral market, and it converts a billing dispute
into something worse.

Collections, if any, run separately through SOP-005 and never through withholding
work product.

## The sequence

### 1. Acknowledge in writing, same day (15 minutes)

1. Confirm receipt of the notice, in writing, the day it arrives.
2. State the **final close month** explicitly, and the delivery date for it.
3. State the **final draft date and amount**, and that no draft will run after it.
4. Ask one question, without pressure and without a save attempt: is there
   anything they want handled differently in the handover.
5. Do not argue, do not counter-offer, and do not ask why more than once. If they
   volunteer a reason, write it down verbatim; it is the most useful data the
   engagement will ever produce.

### 2. Stop the money before anything else (15 minutes)

This is first because it is the one that causes real damage if it slips.

1. **Cancel the ACH autodraft after the final scheduled draft**, and confirm the
   cancellation is actually in place rather than merely requested.
2. Diary the next 1st of the month and verify **no draft fired**. An autodraft
   against a departed client's account is the single worst administrative error
   this firm can make. It is somebody's money taken without authority, and it is
   exactly the accusation the entire operating model is built to be immune to.
3. If a draft fires anyway, refund it the same day you discover it, without
   waiting to be asked, and say so plainly.

### 3. Run the final close properly (SOP-001 and SOP-011, in full)

The last month is closed to exactly the same standard as every other month.

The temptation to run a quick final close is strong and completely wrong. The
final close is the one their next bookkeeper will read, and the one their CPA
will work from. It is the last thing this firm's name is attached to inside that
file.

1. Full SOP-001 close, full SOP-011 detection sweep, no shortcuts.
2. Reconcile everything to zero.
3. Ask My Client is zero, or every remaining item is named and dollar-valued in
   the final summary.
4. Set the closing date through the final close month.

### 4. Produce the handover package (45 minutes)

Deliver through the portal, and confirm receipt.

1. **Final P&L, balance sheet, and plain-words summary** for the last month.
2. **Trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, and general ledger** for the full period
   the firm handled.
3. **All reconciliation reports** for the period.
4. **The client's standing rules document.** This is theirs. It is the most
   valuable thing in the package for whoever comes next, and handing it over
   costs nothing and buys goodwill that outlasts the engagement.
5. **A short handover note**, one page: what state the books are in, what is
   parked and why, the closing date currently set, anything the next bookkeeper
   or the CPA should know, and anything that was flagged and never resolved by
   the client.
6. **Return source documents** the client provided, or confirm in writing that
   they remain available in the portal until a stated date.

### 5. Write to the CPA (10 minutes)

One short note, if the client consents. Books are current through a stated date,
here is what is parked, here is what was flagged and unresolved.

A clean handover is visible to the CPA, and the CPA is a referral source who
outlasts any single client. This is the cheapest reputational insurance
available.

### 6. Close access, on the day it ends, not eventually (20 minutes)

Do all of these on the agreed end date. Not "when there is time."

1. **Remove the firm's accountant access** to the QuickBooks Online file.
2. **Hand over the closing-date password** to the client in writing, or confirm
   the closing date is set and tell them who to ask. They cannot maintain their
   own books through a lock they do not hold.
3. **Revoke any other access** the firm holds: shared folders, document portal,
   any app connected on the client's behalf.
4. **Close the client's Dropbox File Request** so the upload link stops working.
   An orphaned upload link is an open door with nobody watching it.
5. **Delete every credential associated with the engagement** from the password
   manager.
6. Confirm in writing, to the client, that access has been removed and on what
   date. This protects them and it protects us.

### 7. Start the retention clock (15 minutes)

1. The firm's own work product moves to the **encrypted archive** under the
   retention schedule in SOP-007. **Seven years.**
2. Retention is not optional and it is not discretionary. Do not delete the
   archive because the relationship ended. If a question arises in three years
   about work this firm did, the archive is the only answer that exists.
3. Client data does not move anywhere else. Not the repo, not Drive, not the
   Claude Project. (CLAUDE.md hard rule.)

### 8. Record what actually happened (20 minutes)

The part everyone skips, and the only part that improves the business.

1. Close the client's KPI line (SOP-008): total months served, total revenue,
   total hours, effective hourly rate across the whole engagement.
2. Write down **why they left**, in their words if they gave a reason and in
   yours if they did not.
3. Answer honestly: **would we take this client again?** If no, what would have
   told us at screening?
4. If the answer points at a gap in the screening scorecard, fix SOP-010 now.
   That is the entire return on this document.
5. If anything went wrong during the engagement that has not already been
   logged, log it (SOP-011, Part 6).

### 9. Leave the door open (5 minutes)

A final note, short and without a pitch: it was good to work with them, the
handover package is in the portal, and they are welcome back if their situation
changes.

Clients who leave because they hired in-house, or because the business shrank,
or because money got tight, come back at a meaningfully high rate. The ones who
come back are the ones whose last interaction was gracious.

## Termination for cause: what changes

Everything above still happens. **The books still go to the client, in full, on
time.** What changes is the tone and the documentation.

- Keep every communication factual and short. Nothing in writing that would read
  badly if quoted back later, because it may be.
- Document the specific reason for termination and the date.
- If the cause was a client asking for something recorded in a way we would not
  record it: **document what was asked, what was declined, and why**, and get
  advice before responding further. This is a scenario where being the one who
  resigned matters, and the paper trail is the whole defense.
- Never accuse. State what the firm will and will not do, and end it.
- If money is owed, it runs through SOP-005 and never through withholding work
  product.

## Done when

- The notice was acknowledged in writing the same day, with the final close month
  and the final draft named.
- The ACH authorization is cancelled and the following month was verified to
  confirm no draft fired.
- The final close ran to full standard and the period is locked.
- The full handover package is delivered through the portal and receipt is
  confirmed.
- The CPA has been notified, where the client consented.
- Every access the firm held is revoked, on the end date, and confirmed in
  writing to the client.
- The upload link is closed and every credential is deleted.
- The archive is filed and the seven-year retention clock has started.
- The KPI line is closed, the departure reason is recorded, and the
  would-we-again question is answered honestly.
- Anything learned has been promoted into SOP-010 or SOP-011.

## If something goes wrong

- **A draft fires after the engagement ended.** Refund it the same day you find
  it, unprompted, and say so plainly. Then find out why the cancellation did not
  take, and fix the process, not just the instance.
- **The client wants the books but owes money.** They get the books. Collections
  runs separately (SOP-005). This is not negotiable and it is not a close call.
- **The client disputes the quality of the work.** Do not argue by email. Pull the
  archive, pull the reconciliation reports, and look at what actually happened
  first. If we were wrong, say so and fix it, because our published promise is
  that we fix our own mistakes at no charge. If we were not, respond once,
  factually, with the documents.
- **The client asks us to leave the closing date locked and keep the password.**
  Decline. It is their file, and holding a lock on a file we no longer service
  is responsibility without access.
- **The next bookkeeper contacts us.** Be helpful and factual, with the client's
  written consent and not without it. It costs an email and it is exactly the
  behavior that produces referrals from people who never were our clients.
- **They ask us to stay on "just for the year-end."** That is a new engagement.
  It goes through screening and a fresh letter, not a handshake extension of one
  that ended.
