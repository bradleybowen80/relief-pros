# Client Lifecycle

Five checklists covering one client from the hour they sign to the day the
relationship ends. Read in order the first time. After that, each is used on its
own at the point in the relationship it covers.

These are numbered as SOPs because everything in this repo is referenced by
number, and they are grouped in this folder because they are one arc rather than
five unrelated procedures.

| SOP | Covers | When |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-012](SOP-012-day-one.md) | Day one, the first 24 hours after signature | The engagement letter comes back signed |
| [SOP-013](SOP-013-first-30-days.md) | Standing the client up, through the first close | Immediately after day one |
| [SOP-014](SOP-014-first-quarter.md) | The first three closes, proving the system holds | After the first close is delivered |
| [SOP-015](SOP-015-weekly-hour.md) | The steady state, roughly one hour a week | Once the first quarter is behind you |
| [SOP-016](SOP-016-offboarding.md) | Offboarding, whoever ends it | A client leaves, or is not renewed |

## The arc, in one paragraph

Day one is about access and control, not bookkeeping. The first 30 days are
about building a file that is provably correct at a known starting point, which
is the only thing that makes every later month cheap. The first quarter is where
the relationship's real shape appears: whether documents arrive, whether the tier
was priced right, whether this client is a keeper. After that the work should
compress to about an hour a week, and if it has not, something in the first 90
days was skipped.

## The timing rule everything here obeys

The engagement letter and the website both promise **books closed and delivered
by the 15th of the following month**. Every checklist in this folder is built
backward from that date.

Generic bookkeeping checklists found online run a 30-day cycle and deliver at
month end. **Do not adopt one.** A 30-day cycle breaches our published promise
by roughly two weeks, and the promise is a large part of why anyone hires us.

The month, as we run it:

| Days | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 | Documents arrive. Automated requests on the 1st, 3rd, and 5th. |
| 6 to 10 | Categorization. Ideally already mostly done by the weekly feed pass. |
| 11 to 13 | Reconcile, then run the SOP-011 detection sweep. |
| 14 | The gap. Finished work sits overnight and gets reviewed with fresh eyes. |
| 15 | Lock, then deliver. Locking first is what guarantees the delivered reports match the sealed period. |
| 16 to month end | Weekly feed passes so next month's close is short. |

## The load these are built to

Roughly **nine hours per client per month for the first six months**, settling to
about **six to seven** once the file is mature: one hour a week on the file plus a
two to three hour month-end close block. SOP-015 states the steady-state budget
once and the other documents point at it, so if the number ever needs to change
it changes in one place.

The business plan's five-hour figure is the delivery time it models for capacity
planning. Six to seven is the number to schedule against. If a client is still
costing nine hours at month twelve, that is a pricing or screening finding, not a
personal failing. Take it to the KPI review (SOP-008) and the renewal scorecard
(SOP-010).
