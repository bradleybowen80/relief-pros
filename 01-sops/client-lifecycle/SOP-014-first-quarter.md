# SOP-014: The First Quarter

**Status:** active
**Owner:** Sarah
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Purpose

The first three delivered closes. This is where a client stops being a setup
project and becomes either a stable, profitable account or a quiet problem that
will not announce itself for another year.

Month one proves the file can be closed. The quarter proves the file can be
closed **repeatably, on budget, without heroics**. Those are different things,
and only the second one is a business.

The quarter is also the first honest look at whether the engagement was priced
correctly, and the last comfortable moment to say so.

## Trigger

The first close has been delivered. This SOP runs through the third close, whenever that lands on the calendar.

---

## Every month in the quarter

Months two and three run SOP-001 normally, with the SOP-011 sweep, delivered by
the 15th. In addition, do these four things.

### 1. Log hours honestly, every single month

Not an estimate. Not the number that makes the tier look right. The real one.

The budget is roughly **nine hours per client per month for the first six
months**, settling to about **six to seven** once the file is mature (SOP-015).
The whole pricing structure and the capacity ceiling depend on that number being
real. A bookkeeper who rounds their own hours down is lying to the business plan,
and the business plan is what decides when to hire.

Track the trend across the three months. Flat or falling is healthy. Rising is a
finding.

### 2. Watch the question list shrink

Count the Ask My Client items each month. This is the single best leading
indicator in the whole engagement.

| Pattern | What it means |
|---|---|
| Falling month over month | The standing rules are working. This is the expected shape. |
| Flat | The rules are not being written down. Every month you are re-deriving decisions you already made. Fix the standing rules document. |
| Rising | Either the business is changing or the client is getting sloppier about what runs through the account. Ask which. |

### 3. Watch the calendar, not just the close

Log the date each month's documents actually arrived and the date you delivered.
Three months of data tells you whether the 15th is comfortable or whether every
month is a scramble that has so far happened to work out.

A close that lands on the 15th three times because of three late nights is not a
process. It is luck with a good track record.

### 4. Keep writing the standing rules

Every judgment call resolved this month goes into the document. If it came up
once it will come up again, and next time it should take ten seconds instead of
an email.

---

## The quarter-end review

Do this once, after the third close is delivered. Budget an hour. It is the
highest-leverage hour in the first ninety days.

### 5. The file health audit

Beyond the monthly sweep. Run these across all three months at once:

| Check | Looking for |
|---|---|
| **Trial balance, three months side by side** | Any account moving in a way nobody can explain. Consistency is the signal; a category that appears in month three and never before means either the business changed or the rules drifted. |
| **Profit and loss by customer, Not Specified column** | Job costs with no job attached. If this column is growing, job costing is quietly failing. |
| **Bank rules export** | Every rule, its target, and above all its auto-confirm flag. Confirm the client has not added rules. (SOP-011.) |
| **Audit log, full quarter, Events = Changed settings** | Somebody turning off the closing date, changing the fiscal year, or flipping the accounting method. These restate everything and no closing date stops them. |
| **Exceptions to Closing Date, full quarter** | Any closed-period change. Even one means the closing-date control is leaking. |
| **Chart of accounts** | New accounts created by the client mid-quarter. Very common, rarely mentioned, and it fragments the categorization scheme. |
| **Vendor list, Track payments for 1099 flags** | Any new subcontractor added since onboarding without the flag. |
| **The written account list from SOP-012** | Re-confirm it with the client. A new card, processor, or line of credit opened during the quarter and never mentioned is the most common way books that reconcile perfectly are still materially wrong. |
| **Primary admin on the QBO file** | Still the client, still the right person, and no prior bookkeeper still holding it. Whoever holds it can delete the closing date. |
| **Owner's draw account** | Activity. A profitable owner-operated business with a zero draw account means personal spending is being expensed somewhere. (SOP-011.) |

### 6. The pricing and tier review

Answer these plainly and write the answers down.

- **What were the actual hours, per month, all three months?**
- **At the current fee, what is the effective hourly rate?**
- **Is the client inside the tier they bought?** Count the accounts actually
  reconciled and the transaction volume actually processed against what the tier
  says.
- **Is the client trending toward the next tier?** Growing businesses add
  accounts and volume quietly.
- **Did anything get done for free this quarter that is not in the engagement?**

If the client is outside their tier, the conversation happens **now**, at the
quarter, framed as growth rather than as a complaint. "You've added two accounts
and your volume is up 40%, which puts you in Standard starting next month" is
easy at ninety days. The same sentence at month ten sounds like a firm that
cannot count.

If the answer is that this client is simply unprofitable at any tier, that is a
screening finding. Take it to SOP-010 and fix the scorecard so the next one is
caught before signature.

### 7. The client relationship review

- Are they reading the summaries? Ask directly.
- Are documents arriving in the first five days, or does every month need a
  chase?
- Have they asked for anything outside scope, and was it declined cleanly and
  in writing?
- Would you want ten more clients like this one?

That last question is the whole point of the review. The book you build in the
first year is the book you live with, and a two-person firm cannot outrun a bad
fit by working harder.

### 8. The review request

If all three closes were clean and on time, the review request goes out. Use
[04-clients/review-request-template.md](../../04-clients/review-request-template.md).

Read its compliance rules before sending. In short: never offer anything in
exchange, never mention the referral bonus in the same message, ask every
qualifying client rather than only the ones you expect to be happy, and never
direct what the review says.

If any of the three closes was late, required an apology, or parked something
material, **do not send it.** Fix the thing and ask next quarter.

### 9. The CPA touch

One short email to the client's CPA. Three months of clean books is a
credibility event, and it is the moment to spend it.

- Confirm the books are current and reconciled through the quarter.
- Surface anything they should know: unverified opening balances, items above
  the capitalization threshold, potential 1099 vendors, anything ambiguous you
  deliberately did not resolve.
- Ask whether the delivery format is working for them.

The business plan identifies CPA referrals as a dominant acquisition channel.
This email is how that channel actually gets built: not by asking for referrals,
but by being visibly easy to work with three months in a row.

### 10. Sarah's judgment log review

Read back every judgment call escalated to Sarah in the quarter. Two questions:

1. Which of these should have been a standing rule instead of a question?
2. Which revealed something about the client's business the file should reflect
   structurally?

Then update the standing rules and, if a pattern shows up across clients, promote
it into SOP-011's detection sweep. **A protocol that never changes is a protocol
nobody is learning from.**

## Done when

- Three closes delivered, all reconciled to zero, all on or before the 15th.
- Hours logged honestly for all three months, with the trend noted.
- The Ask My Client count is falling, or the reason it is not has been diagnosed
  and addressed.
- The file health audit is complete with findings written down.
- The tier is confirmed correct, or the tier conversation has already happened.
- The standing rules document has grown materially since day 30.
- The review request went out, or the specific reason it did not is recorded.
- The CPA has had one substantive, useful contact.
- Anything learned has been promoted into the standing rules or into SOP-011.

## If something goes wrong

- **Hours are not falling by month three.** Something structural is unresolved.
  Usually the chart of accounts, the standing rules, or a client whose documents
  arrive in a shape that fights the system. Diagnose it now; it does not improve
  on its own.
- **The client is outside their tier.** Have the conversation this quarter.
  Framed as growth, at ninety days, it lands well.
- **The client is unprofitable at any tier.** Screening finding. Fix SOP-010.
  Decide at renewal whether to keep them, and be honest that "we like them" is
  not a financial argument.
- **A closed period was changed.** Find out who and how. If the client has the
  closing-date password, they should not. If the change is material, it may need
  to go to the CPA.
- **The client keeps asking for out-of-scope work.** Each decline in writing.
  A pattern of asking is a renewal-scorecard item, not a personality quirk.
- **You would not want ten more like this.** Say so out loud at the review and
  write down why. That sentence is worth more than the rest of this checklist.
