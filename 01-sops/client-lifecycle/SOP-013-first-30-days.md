# SOP-013: The First 30 Days

**Status:** active
**Owner:** Sarah
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Purpose

Take a client from "signed and set up" to "first close delivered on time, from a
starting point we can prove."

Everything expensive about a bookkeeping engagement is decided here. A file with
verified opening balances, a clean chart of accounts, and standing rules written
down settles into the steady-state budget in SOP-015, roughly six to seven hours
a month. The same client without those never gets there, and the fee never
changes to match.

The plan budgets roughly **nine hours per client per month for the first six
months**, which is what the setup work in this document costs. That is the
budget, not a warning sign. It becomes a warning sign only if it is still nine
hours at month twelve.

## Trigger

SOP-012 is complete. The welcome email has gone out.

## Stage 1, before the first document window opens: build the foundation

Nothing in this stage involves categorizing a transaction. Resist it. Categorizing
before the foundation is set means categorizing twice, and it is the single most
common and most expensive cleanup mistake there is.

### 1. Audit the chart of accounts, before anything else

1. Read the whole chart of accounts before touching a transaction.
2. Standardize naming and merge duplicates. **Before deactivating any balance
   sheet account, confirm its balance is zero.** If it is not, QuickBooks Online
   posts an automatic adjustment to Opening Balance Equity to zero it out, which
   restates the file and produces exactly the non-zero OBE that SOP-011 treats as
   a hard stop. Clear the balance to its proven account first, then deactivate.
3. **Merging accounts moves every historical transaction and is not reversible.**
   Never merge across a closed period without Sarah's approval and a dated
   baseline export.
4. For a contractor: confirm **direct job costs sit in cost of goods sold**, not
   in overhead, and that **materials and subcontractors are separate accounts**.
   Materials buried in "Office Supplies" produces a gross margin that looks
   wonderful and means nothing.
5. Confirm every account's **type and detail type** are right. Detail type drives
   QBO behavior, and a wrong one is invisible until a report is wrong.
6. Check whether **inventory tracking is on**. Most contractors should not have
   it. Materials bought for a job are job costs, not inventory. If it is on and
   the client is not a real stocking operation, that is a structural finding to
   raise now, not later.

### 2. Verify opening balances and prove them

1. Establish the engagement start date and the opening balances as of that date.
2. Tie **every** opening bank, credit card, and loan balance to a statement or a
   lender balance dated that day. Not approximately. Exactly.
3. Confirm **Opening Balance Equity nets to zero** once setup is complete. A
   non-zero balance is a setup error and it gets resolved now, not at the first
   close, because every future period inherits it.
4. Where the client's prior-year tax return exists, tie the opening balance sheet
   to the return. Where it does not, mark the opening balances **unverified** in
   writing and tell the CPA. Never invent one to make the file look finished.

### 3. Set the client's standing rules with Sarah

1. Sit down once and decide: how this client's recurring vendors are categorized,
   what counts as a job cost versus overhead, how owner spending is handled, and
   anything idiosyncratic about their business.
2. Write it in the standing rules document. Every future month is executed
   against this file rather than re-derived, and it is what makes the work
   transferable to a hire.
3. Get the **capitalization threshold** answer back from the CPA and record it.

### 4. Build the bank rules, carefully

1. Configure rules for genuinely recurring, unambiguous transactions.
2. **Auto-confirm stays off** except for rigid, fixed-amount, single-payee items:
   rent, a named loan draft, a fixed insurance payment. Not fuel. Not building
   supply stores. (SOP-011, Rule 4.)
3. Set rule **priority** deliberately. A broad rule above a specific one silently
   swallows what the specific rule was written for.
4. Delete or fix any rule the client built that conflicts with the standing rules.

### 5. Collect the W-9s now, in month one

Every subcontractor and 1099-eligible vendor. Now, while the client is engaged
and cooperative, not in January when they are not.

This is counter-seasonal by design. It removes the January scramble and lets the
client's CPA prepare the 1099s from clean, complete vendor data instead of from a
reconstruction. **We collect the W-9s and set the coding. We do not prepare or
file the returns.**

While you are in the vendor list, check the **Track payments for 1099** flag on
every vendor with recurring payments. A subcontractor paid from an unmapped
account or an unflagged vendor silently disappears from the 1099 report. We do
not file the returns; the coding decides whether anyone can.

Note the threshold changed: **$600 for tax year 2025, $2,000 for tax year 2026.**

## Stage 2, during the first document window: the first real month of work

### 6. Categorize with the standing rules, deliberately

1. Work one account at a time.
2. **Add versus Match is decided by a person, every transaction.** QBO looks back
   roughly ninety days and guesses.
3. Attach the source document to the transaction as you go. QuickBooks Online
   has receipt capture built in; there is no need to buy a separate tool.
4. Anything you cannot prove goes to **Ask My Client**. Never a plausible guess.
   (SOP-011, Rule 1.)

### 7. Run the first question list early, not at the deadline

Send the Ask My Client list to the client **by the 10th of the close month**, not on the 14th.

The first month is when a client learns what kind of client they are going to
be. If the first list arrives with a day to spare and a note about why it
matters, most people answer. If it arrives the night before delivery, they learn
that questions are urgent noise, and that habit lasts for years.

### 8. Reconcile everything, to zero

1. Every bank account, then every credit card, then anything else reconciled.
2. Difference must be exactly **0.00** against the real statement.
3. A difference is diagnosed through the Reconcile Discrepancy Report or
   escalated. It is **never** plugged. (SOP-011, Rule 2.)
4. Save each reconciliation report to the client folder. It is the proof.

### 9. Run the full SOP-011 detection sweep

All fourteen checks. On the first close, run them even where you are confident,
because the first close is where inherited problems surface.

## Stage 3, the first close: deliver, and set the pattern

### 10. The gap

The close is finished, then it sits, then it is reviewed with fresh eyes before
it goes out. Not delivered in the same sitting it was completed in.

With one bookkeeper this is not optional politeness, it is one of the few real
review controls the firm has. (SOP-011, Part 2.)

### 11. Deliver by the 15th

Profit and loss, balance sheet, and a **plain-words summary**, through the portal
only, never email.

The first summary sets the tone for every one after it. It should say, in
language an owner reads at a glance: what the numbers say, what changed, what is
parked and why, and anything they need to do. No jargon. No em dashes.

If anything is parked because a document never arrived, name it and give the
dollar amount. The close goes out on time regardless. That is firm policy stated
in the engagement letter, not a negotiation held every month.

### 12. Lock the period

Closing date set, password held by the firm, confirmed before moving on.

### 13. Archive and log

1. Export the month's trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet, and general
   ledger to the encrypted archive. This proves the firm's work independently of
   continued QBO access or the client relationship.
2. Add the client to the KPI dashboard (SOP-008) with hours actually spent. Log
   the real number, not the number you wish it were. The whole pricing model
   depends on this being honest.
3. Log anything that went wrong in the error log (SOP-011, Part 6).

### 14. The post-first-close check-in

A short call or note. Three questions:

1. Is the summary useful, or would something else be more useful?
2. Is the document upload working for you?
3. Anything about your business changing in the next few months that we should
   know about?

Not a sales call. Question 3 in particular catches a client outgrowing their
tier before it becomes an unpriced problem.

## Done when

- Chart of accounts is clean, correctly typed, and structured for the client's
  actual business.
- Every opening balance ties to an external document, or is explicitly marked
  unverified in writing to the CPA.
- Opening Balance Equity is zero.
- Standing rules exist in writing, including the CPA's capitalization threshold.
- Bank rules are built, prioritized, and auto-confirm is off except where
  explicitly justified.
- W-9s are collected and the 1099 vendor flags are checked.
- The first close reconciled to zero on every account, passed the full SOP-011
  sweep, and was delivered by the 15th.
- The period is locked and the firm holds the password.
- The month is archived, hours are logged honestly, and the 30-day check-in
  happened.

## If something goes wrong

- **Opening balances cannot be proven.** Do not invent them. Document what is
  unverified, tell the CPA in writing, and carry the flag until it is resolved.
- **The file needs more work than the engagement covers.** Stop and re-quote as a
  cleanup (SOP-003). Month one is the last easy moment to have this
  conversation; by month three it sounds like an excuse.
- **Documents do not arrive.** Park, disclose, deliver on time, log the client on
  the document chase list (SOP-004). Do not delay a close waiting on a client.
  Do not absorb the chase silently either; it goes on the KPI line.
- **The client is already at double the budgeted hours.** Log it now and flag it
  for the renewal scorecard (SOP-010). A tier mismatch caught in month one is a
  conversation. Caught in month nine it is a resentment.
- **A transaction needs a judgment call Brad cannot make.** Sarah's queue. It
  does not get guessed to keep the queue clean. (SOP-011, Level 3.)
