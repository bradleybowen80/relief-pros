# SOP-015: The Weekly Hour

**Status:** active
**Owner:** Sarah
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Purpose

The steady state. Day 91 onward, for as long as the client is a client.

**The steady-state budget, stated once so every other document can point here:
roughly one hour a week on the file, plus a two to three hour month-end close
block. Call it six to seven hours a month.**

The first six months run higher, around nine hours a month, because the chart of
accounts, the standing rules, and the client's habits are all still being built.
Nine hours in month twelve is a finding, not a budget.

The business plan's five-hour steady-state figure is the *delivery* time it
models for capacity planning. Treat six to seven as the real number when you are
scheduling a week, and treat the gap between them as the margin that the weekly
rhythm is supposed to earn back.

## Why weekly, and not "do it all at month end"

Because a month of transactions touched once is a cliff, and cliffs are where
errors live.

Categorizing four weeks of a contractor's spending in one sitting on the 11th
means deciding hundreds of items with no memory of any of them, under time
pressure, against a delivery date three days out. That is precisely the state in
which a person guesses a category rather than asking, accepts a match rather than
checking it, and plugs a difference rather than diagnosing it.

Touching the file weekly means every transaction is decided while it is recent,
every question reaches the client with weeks of runway instead of hours, and the
month-end close is a verification exercise rather than a production run. The
close gets shorter, the quality goes up, and the deadline stops being a threat.

This is the mechanism by which nine hours a month becomes six or seven.

## The hour

Same day every week. Put it on the calendar as a real appointment. A rhythm that
depends on remembering is not a rhythm.

### Block 1: the feed pass (30 minutes)

1. Open **Bank transactions** and work one account at a time.
2. Categorize the week's transactions against the client's standing rules.
3. **Add versus Match, decided every time.** Never batch-accept. QBO looks back
   roughly ninety days and guesses, and a same-amount coincidence is not a match.
4. Attach the source document as you go.
5. Anything you cannot prove goes to **Ask My Client**. Never a plausible guess.
6. Anything a bank rule categorized that looks wrong gets fixed **and** the rule
   gets fixed. A bad rule left alone re-creates the error every week.

If this block regularly runs long, the standing rules are incomplete or the
client's volume has outgrown their tier. Both are findings, not personal
failings.

### Block 2: the question sweep (15 minutes)

1. Review what landed in Ask My Client this week.
2. Add each item to the client's running question list with the date, amount,
   and vendor.
3. Send the list. Weekly is better than monthly: shorter lists get answered, and
   a client asked on the 8th about a purchase from the 5th usually remembers.
4. Reclassify anything answered since last week and confirm the account moves
   toward zero.
5. If an item has been outstanding **three weeks**, escalate the tone: it will be
   parked and named in the monthly summary if it is not answered. Then do exactly
   that. Consequences that arrive as promised are what make the next month
   easier.

### Block 3: the anomaly check (15 minutes)

Not the full month-end sweep. Four fast looks for things that should not be true
mid-month:

1. **Ask My Client balance** trending toward zero, not growing.
2. **Uncategorized Asset, Income, and Expense** at zero.
3. **A negative-balance glance** at the balance sheet and profit and loss: any
   negative bank balance, any negative expense account, any negative accounts
   receivable. (SOP-011 has the full table of what each one means.)
4. **Anything unusual for this client**: a payment far outside their normal
   range, a vendor that has never appeared before, a weekend transaction at a
   building supply or fuel vendor. The last one is the highest-yield filter there
   is for personal spending on a business card.

Catching these weekly means they are one transaction to fix. Catching them at
close means they are a pattern to unwind.

## The month-end block, on top of the weekly hour

Days 11 to 15. This is SOP-001, run in full, with the SOP-011 detection sweep.
It should be short, because the weekly hour has already done the categorization.

| Day | What |
|---|---|
| 1, 3, 5 | Automated document requests fire (SOP-004). No manual work. |
| 6 to 10 | Confirm documents are complete. Chase what is missing. |
| 11 to 13 | Reconcile everything to zero. Run the full SOP-011 sweep. On a file where the weekly hour is actually happening this is two to three hours of work inside that window, not three full days. |
| 14 | **The gap.** Finished work sits overnight and is reviewed with fresh eyes. |
| 15 | Lock the period. Deliver through the portal. Archive. Log hours honestly. |

**The gap on day 14 is not padding.** With one bookkeeper it is one of the few
genuine review controls the firm has, and it catches a real share of what a
second reviewer would. Protect it. If day 14 keeps getting eaten, the schedule
is wrong, not the control.

## What does not belong in the weekly hour

Deliberately out of scope, so the hour stays an hour:

- **Reconciling.** That is the month-end block, against a real statement. Mid-month
  reconciling against an incomplete period is busywork that feels productive.
- **Resolving a judgment call.** Brad never does. Sarah's queue, in writing.
  (SOP-011, Level 3.)
- **Chasing collections, working accounts receivable or payable, disputing with
  vendors.** Out of scope entirely, for three separate reasons. AP and AR carry a
  Texas sales tax exposure as data processing (SOP-009, Guardrail 2). Collections
  is separately enumerated and taxable as a debt collection service, which is a
  different provision. And all of them are the highest scope-creep services
  there are. Aging is reviewed for bookkeeping accuracy. It is never worked.
- **Answering tax questions.** Route to the CPA, in writing, every time. (SOP-011,
  Rule 7.)
- **Absorbing new work.** A client who has started sending three times the volume
  is a tier conversation, not a longer Tuesday.

## The overlays

The weekly hour is the base. These sit on top, on their own cadence.

### Monthly

- The close block above.
- Log hours honestly on the KPI line (SOP-008). The real number.
- Log any error caught, and how it got in (SOP-011, Part 6).

### Quarterly

- **Export and audit the bank rules.** Every rule, its target account, its
  assigned customer or project, and its auto-confirm flag. Confirm the client has
  not added any.
- **Exceptions to Closing Date** for the quarter. Any row means the closing-date
  control is leaking.
- **Audit log, Events = Changed settings.** Catches someone turning off the
  closing date, changing the fiscal year, or flipping the accounting method.
  These restate everything and no closing date stops them.
- **Chart of accounts diff.** New accounts the client created without telling
  anyone.
- **Re-confirm the written account list** from SOP-012. A card or processor
  opened mid-year and never mentioned defeats every other control in this SOP.
- **Confirm primary admin on the QBO file** has not changed hands.
- **Tier check.** Accounts actually reconciled and volume actually processed
  against what the tier says.
- **Texas franchise tax and PIR awareness.** The Public Information Report is due
  May 15 regardless of whether any tax is owed, and it is the filing that keeps
  the entity in good standing. We do not file it. We make sure the numbers are
  clean and that the client and the CPA know the date is coming. Q1 is the right
  time to raise it.

### Annually

- **W-9 refresh** for new subcontractors, and a check of the **Track payments for
  1099** flag on every vendor with recurring payments. Done in the fall, not in
  January. The threshold is $2,000 for tax year 2026, filed the following
  January.
- **Year-end handoff to the CPA**, in the format they asked for at onboarding.
- **Standing rules review.** A year of accumulated decisions, read once, cleaned
  up.
- **Renewal screening** against SOP-010. Not automatic. A client who has drifted
  into chronic lateness, chronic scope pressure, or chronic unprofitability gets
  a decision, not a default.
- **Insurance renewal**, with the retroactive date preserved. A claims-made
  policy with a broken retroactive date is a policy that does not cover the work
  it was bought for.

## Signals the hour is not enough

Any of these means something structural, not that you need to work faster.

| Signal | What it usually is |
|---|---|
| The feed pass runs past 45 minutes most weeks | Volume has outgrown the tier, or the standing rules are thin |
| The Ask My Client list stops shrinking | Decisions are being re-derived instead of written down |
| Documents need chasing every single month | A client-behavior problem. Document chase (SOP-004), then the renewal scorecard |
| The close consumes most of days 11 through 15 | The weekly hour is not actually happening, or the file has an unresolved structural problem |
| Day 14 keeps getting consumed | The schedule is overloaded. This is a capacity signal and it arrives before the crisis does |
| The same error keeps appearing | It belongs in the standing rules or in SOP-011's sweep. Promote it. |

The business plan puts the **solo delivery ceiling at roughly 15 clients**. These
signals are how that ceiling announces itself early, months before it is a
crisis, while there is still time to raise prices, tighten screening, or hire.

---

## Done when

Weekly:

- Every transaction from the week is categorized or in Ask My Client.
- The question list went out and answered items were reclassified.
- The anomaly check is clean or has a written finding.

Monthly:

- SOP-001 close delivered by the 15th, period locked, month archived, hours
  logged honestly.

Quarterly and annually:

- The overlays above ran on schedule, with findings written down rather than
  noticed and forgotten.

## If something goes wrong

- **A week gets missed.** Do the next one normally and add the backlog to it
  once. Do not let two missed weeks become the new pattern; that is exactly how a
  file slides back toward nine hours a month.
- **The client's business changes materially.** New entity, new bank accounts, a
  first employee, a first big contract. Stop and reassess the tier and the
  standing rules before absorbing it. Growth absorbed quietly is unpriced work.
- **A closed period changed.** Find out who and how. If the client holds the
  closing-date password, they should not.
- **You find an error in a delivered month.** Tell the client the same day you
  know. Correct it, re-deliver, log it. Our published promise is that we fix our
  own mistakes at no charge, and honoring it before anyone has to ask is most of
  the reason it is worth promising.
- **The client asks for something outside scope.** Decline in writing, refer where
  appropriate, and log the ask. Patterns matter at renewal.
