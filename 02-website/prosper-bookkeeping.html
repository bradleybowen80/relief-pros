Who we work with here

The businesses Prosper actually runs on.

Prosper is not one economy, it is three, and they need different things from their books.

  • The trades and home services. With rooftops still going up across the west side and new sections opening constantly, builders, landscapers, pool crews, HVAC, and electrical are booked solid. Their books live and die on tracking subcontractors and keeping materials separated from everything else. That is our specialty, and we built a whole page for it.
  • The retail and restaurant corridor. The stretch around Preston and 380 keeps adding storefronts. Retail and food bring higher transaction counts, more accounts to reconcile, and a real need to see monthly numbers rather than guessing until tax season.
  • Professional practices and services. Dentists, medical practices, agencies, consultants, studios, and the family-services businesses filling the newer centers. These tend to be clean, steady books, which is exactly the kind of client our Essentials and Standard plans were priced for.

The through line: Prosper businesses are growing faster than their back office. The bookkeeping that worked when it was you and one crew stops working at three crews and four accounts, and it usually stops working quietly, six months before anyone notices.

A local reality worth planning around

Growth is good for revenue and hard on records.

Every business we talk to in this part of the corridor is dealing with some version of the same thing. Revenue is up. Headcount is up. The number of bank and credit card accounts crept up too, because a second card made sense at the time. Somewhere in there, the monthly habit of reconciling everything slipped, and now the books are a few months behind and nobody wants to be the one to open them.

That is not a discipline problem. It is a capacity problem, and it is the single most common reason businesses call us. Catching up is a defined, flat-price project, not an open-ended hourly bill. And once you are current, staying current is a monthly fee smaller than most Prosper businesses spend on software.

One more thing worth knowing if your LLC is a few years old: Texas requires an annual Public Information Report even in years when you owe no franchise tax at all, and the revenue figure behind all of it comes out of your books. We wrote up what that filing is and why owners miss it.
