No mystery, no hand-waving

What we actually do, in the order we do it.

Most cleanup quotes are a price and a shrug. Here is the real sequence, because the order matters more than most people realize.

1

Back up first, lock what's good

We export your file exactly as it stands before touching anything, and protect any period that is already correct so cleanup cannot disturb it.

2

Fix the chart of accounts

Wrong account types, duplicates, and categories nobody uses get corrected before a single transaction is touched. This is the foundation everything else sits on.

3

Merge duplicate vendors

"Home Depot," "Home Depot #4412," and "HomeDepot" are one vendor. Duplicates quietly wreck both your reports and your 1099 totals.

4

Rebuild the bank feeds

Broken connections reconnected, missing history imported, so the transaction set is actually complete before anyone starts categorizing.

5

Now categorize

Uncategorized income and expense worked down to zero against real documents. Never bulk-accepted, never guessed.

6

Chase down the weird stuff

Negative balances, stale items sitting for months, amounts that make no sense. Each one gets diagnosed rather than written off.

7

Reconcile, oldest to newest

Every account matched to real statements, in order, to a zero difference. If something does not tie, we find out why. We never plug a number to make it balance.

8

Tie out loans and cards

Balances matched to what the lender says you actually owe, with interest and principal split correctly.

9

Compare to what you filed

The cleaned numbers get checked against your filed returns. Differences get flagged to you and your CPA, not quietly buried.

10

Lock it and hand it over

Closing date set with a password so the cleaned months stay clean, everything exported, and a summary of what was wrong and what we fixed.

💡 Why the order matters: the most common and most expensive cleanup mistake is diving straight into categorizing transactions before the chart of accounts, the vendor list, and the bank feeds are fixed. Do it in that order and you categorize thousands of transactions twice. We have seen it, and it is why step 5 is step 5 and not step 1.

Straight answers about the hard part

How behind is too behind?

Short answer: there is no such thing. There is only what it costs and how long it takes. Books that are three months behind are a tidy project. Books that are three years behind with a closed bank account and missing statements are a different animal, and we will say so in writing before you spend a dollar.

What actually makes a cleanup harder is not the number of months. It is missing records. Statements you can still download, we can work with. Accounts that are closed with no statements available are where projects get slow, because the transactions have to be rebuilt from whatever evidence still exists. If you are thinking about closing an old business account, download everything first. That is free advice and it will save you real money later.
