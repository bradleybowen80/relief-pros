The three ways cleanup gets priced

Hourly, per-month-behind, or by project. Know which you're buying.

  • Hourly: commonly $50–$100+ an hour, open-ended. The most common and the most dangerous way to buy cleanup, because messy books are exactly the job nobody can estimate in advance. The bill is whatever it ends up being, and you find out at the end.
  • Per month behind: commonly $195–$495 for each month of history. National catch-up services price this way. It's more predictable than hourly, but do the math: eight months behind at $300 a month is $2,400 before your first normal month.
  • Flat project fee: quoted up front. The honest version of cleanup pricing, if the quote is written and the scope is clear. Published project fees in our own area run as high as $7,500–$10,000 at one firm. Ours is below.

Why the market spread is so huge

Same phrase, very different jobs.

"Cleanup" has no fixed definition, which is how one owner pays $400 and another pays $8,000 for what sounds like the same thing. The price differences come from real questions: How many months? How many accounts? Are the statements available? Is history miscategorized or just missing? Does the file need repair, or does it need to be rebuilt? A firm quoting without asking these questions is guessing, and open-ended hourly billing means their guess becomes your problem.

One more honest thing most firms won't say: sometimes the right answer is a fresh QuickBooks file, not a cleanup. If the file is beyond saving, starting over is genuinely cheaper. We'll tell you which side of that line you're on before you pay anyone anything. That advice costs nothing and it will save you real money.
