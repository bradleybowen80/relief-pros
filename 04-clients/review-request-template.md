# Review Request Template

**Status:** active
**Owner:** Sarah
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-31

## Purpose

Ask for a Google review in a way that is effective, repeatable, and compliant.
Reviews are the single highest-leverage local ranking and conversion factor for
a service-area business, and they are the one thing a brand new firm cannot buy,
fake, or shortcut. This template is what turns a clean close into a review
without turning the firm into a nuisance.

This satisfies SOP-001 step 12.3, which triggers a review request after a clean
close but had no template behind it.

## The compliance rules, read these before using anything below

These are not style preferences. Violating them risks review removal, profile
suspension, or an FTC enforcement action, and it is the kind of thing that is
hard to unwind once it is in the record.

1. **Never offer anything in exchange for a review.** No discount, no credit, no
   gift card, no free month. Google's Rating Manipulation policy prohibits
   incentivized reviews outright. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials
   Rule, effective October 21, 2024, authorizes civil penalties for knowing
   violations. This one is absolute.
2. **The $250 referral bonus and a review are never mentioned in the same
   message and never conditioned on each other.** Paying for a referred customer
   is legal and normal. Paying for a review is not. Keeping those two
   conversations physically separate is what keeps the distinction obvious to a
   regulator reading our emails later.
3. **No review gating.** Do not ask only the clients you expect to be happy, and
   do not run a "how did we do?" screen that routes happy clients to Google and
   unhappy clients to a private form. Google explicitly bans selectively
   soliciting customers expected to leave positive feedback. Every client on the
   list below gets asked, on the same schedule, with the same words.
4. **Do not direct the content of the review.** Do not ask for a specific rating,
   do not supply talking points, and do not ask the client to mention a staff
   member by name. Google's April 2026 update banned directing staff to request
   reviews naming a staff member, and asking a client to do it is the same act
   from the other side. "Tell people whatever is true for you" is the whole ask.
5. **No quotas.** Nobody in this firm is ever given a target number of reviews to
   collect. Google banned that explicitly in April 2026.
6. **Never threaten or pressure a client over a negative review.** The FTC rule
   prohibits using false accusations, intimidation, or groundless legal threats
   to get a review removed. The only correct response to a bad review is a
   public, calm, specific reply and a private attempt to fix the thing.

## Trigger and cadence

| When | What goes out |
|---|---|
| After the **second** consecutive clean close, delivered on time | Ask #1, the email below |
| **Seven days later**, only if no review has appeared | Ask #2, the short follow-up |
| After that | Nothing. Never a third ask for that cycle |
| **Cleanup delivery** | Ask #1, sent the day the cleanup is delivered. This is the highest-relief moment in the whole relationship and the strongest review we will ever get |
| Ongoing | At most **one review cycle per client per twelve months** |

Why the second close and not the first: after one close a client is satisfied,
after two they are confident, and confidence is what produces a review that
mentions consistency. It is also proof to us that the engagement actually works
before we ask the client to vouch for it publicly.

Send from Sarah, never from a bulk mail tool. A review request that arrives
looking like marketing automation gets treated like marketing automation.

## Do not send if

- The close was late, parked a material item, or required an apology.
- The client has an open complaint or an unresolved question.
- The client is behind on documents and knows it. Asking a client for a favor
  while chasing them for statements reads badly and lands badly.
- A payment failed this month (SOP-005 is running).

A clean close means clean. If it was not, fix the thing and ask next cycle.

## Responding to reviews, which matters as much as getting them

Respond to every review, positive and negative, within two business days.
Responses are public, they are read by prospects far more than people assume,
and Google explicitly permits and expects them.

**Positive review response, keep it short and specific and never generic:**

> Thank you, [NAME]. Getting the books closed by the 15th every month is the
> whole promise, so it means a lot to hear it is landing that way. We appreciate
> you.

**Negative review response.** Read it twice, wait an hour, then write. Never
argue, never mention anything client-confidential, never imply the client is
wrong in public even when they are.

> [NAME], thank you for telling us plainly. [One sentence naming the specific
> thing that went wrong, with no excuse attached.] That is not the standard we
> hold ourselves to. I would like to make it right, and I am reaching out
> directly today. Sarah Bowen, 469-702-1185.

Then actually call. Then log it in the failure log so the SOP gets fixed, not
just the review.

**Never** respond to a negative review by disputing facts, citing the engagement
letter, or referencing anything about the client's finances. Confidentiality does
not lapse because someone criticized us in public.

## If a review is fake or violates policy

Report it through the Google Business Profile dashboard and let the process run.
Do not contact the reviewer to pressure a removal. Do not have friends or family
post offsetting reviews, which is itself a policy violation and is detectable.
The FTC rule specifically prohibits using intimidation or groundless legal
threats to get reviews taken down.

## Where the review link lives

The review link is generated in the Google Business Profile dashboard once the
profile is verified. Store it in [citation-pack.md](../03-marketing/citation-pack.md)
so there is one canonical copy and nobody retypes it. Blocked until the pending
video verification clears.

## After sending

1. Log the ask date on the client's KPI line (SOP-008) so nobody double-asks.
2. Log whether a review resulted, and its rating. Review rate per ask is a real
   operating metric and belongs on the dashboard.
3. Never log or store the review request against anything containing client
   financial data.
